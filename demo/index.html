Main finding: more actual prices fell inside our predicted ranges. Widening the ranges raised historical coverage from 64.70% to 75.42%, toward the 80% goal. The original autonomous loop did not establish a reliable gain. Why widen the ranges? In 52.8% of the original predictions, the lower and upper prices were identical, leaving no room for a price change. The follow-up widened the ranges without changing the middle predictions.

Selected wider ranges versus Pacific: the measured comparison

This chart compares prediction errors, not GPU prices. Each past prediction is scored against the price later recorded. Green shows predictions after broadening their price ranges; blue shows the same predictions before that adjustment. We lowered the low estimate and raised the high estimate, keeping the middle prediction unchanged. Each point combines all tests up to that date. The widening rule was designed after seeing the original errors: this is a retrospective comparison, not a new untouched test.

Three ideas the agents tested
1. Use recent prices: learn from the last 28 or 56 days instead of the full history.
2. Allow for prices that stay fixed: predict smaller changes when rental prices rarely move.
3. Adjust the price range: allow more room when prices fluctuate or earlier ranges missed the actual price.
Both lines replay measured historical results. The final green point matches the +4.323% result above, whose uncertainty interval includes no improvement. Replaying does not run a new experiment.

2028–29 outlook · Assumptions, not tested future results

By 2029, our central scenario predicts H100 rents falling from $3.55 to $0.98–$2.59 and A100 rents from $1.95 to $0.42–$1.20 per GPU-hour.

Buyers may pay less for a used GPU if they expect to make less profit renting it out.

In selected public MLCommons tests compiled by Pacific, H100 processed about 2.46 times as much work per second per GPU as A100 when running trained AI models.
